# Corpus DNA — ADAS reference set (35 scripts)

This reference was derived from **all 35 scripts** in the supplied archive, not from a small sample.

## Quantitative baseline
- Scripts analyzed: **35**
- Metadata records with duration/views: **31**
- Median clean-script length: **40,438 Japanese characters**
- Script-length range: **28,860–45,778 characters**
- Median runtime: **113.2 minutes**
- Runtime range: **83.3–130.2 minutes**
- Median narration density: **353 Japanese characters/minute**
- Median title length: **90 characters** (range 55–99)

## Title DNA across all 35
- Family/relationship term present: **29/35 (82.9%)**
- Direct quotation 「…」 present: **26/35 (74.3%)**
- `瞬間` present: **15/35 (42.9%)**
- `ところが` present: **10/35 (28.6%)**
- Concrete money figure present: **10/35 (28.6%)**
- `億` present: **6/35 (17.1%)**

## Strongest observed construction patterns
1. **Relationship first, spectacle second.** The strongest premises usually attach the mystery to spouse/parent/child/step-parent/sibling/in-law relationships.
2. **Concrete proof object.** Letter, bank record, key, photo, luggage, bed, filter, air-conditioner cover, old box, medical paper, envelope, money amount.
3. **Delayed answer.** The title promises a moment; the script opens on/near that tension, then returns to context and delays the complete explanation.
4. **Information ladder.** Fact → provisional explanation → contradictory clue → deeper fact → meaning reversal.
5. **Moral/emotional reversal can outperform wealth reversal.** The best-performing examples include 28万円, a stepmother's hidden sacrifice, adoption after family loss, and a hidden object under a bed—not only billionaire reveals.
6. **Object callback.** A concrete object introduced early returns near the end with changed meaning.
7. **Quiet protagonist often beats loud protagonist.** Restraint creates dramatic irony: protagonist or ally knows more than antagonist.

## Risks visible in the corpus
- Repeated billionaire/CEO/`億` escalations become predictable.
- Some endings continue too long after the main promised payoff.
- Some scripts rely on a powerful person appearing conveniently instead of a seeded causal chain.
- ASR/transcription text contains name/word corruption; final production needs a Japanese continuity and spoken-language pass.
- Repeated stock phrases (`凍りついた`, `言葉を失った`, `その瞬間`) can become mechanical if used as a substitute for specific reaction.

## Recommended improvement target
Keep the market DNA but improve story quality via:
- 3-layer reveal: **fact → deeper fact → meaning**
- setup/payoff fairness
- explicit continuity ledger
- realistic money/legal/medical/corporate causality
- antagonist motive beyond “evil because plot needs evil”
- final consequence + callback within 10–15% after climax
- no unexplained knowledge, coincidence, inheritance, status, legal authority, or money transfer.
